\begin{abstract}
A disclosed economic value of equity nets an asset valuation that filings constrain
tightly against a deposit valuation they do not constrain at all. We examine that
asymmetry by rebuilding the interest-rate exposure of Silicon Valley Bank and the
Charles Schwab Corporation from public filings and public curve data, calibrating
entirely on 31~December 2021 information and then freezing the option-adjusted
spreads. Priced a year forward, the model returns 80.36 per \$100 of carrying amount
for SVB's held-to-maturity portfolio against the 83.41 disclosed, and $-1.75\%$
error for Schwab's once its available-for-sale transfers are undone. It reproduces
SVB's published sensitivity profile at shock points it was not fitted to, returning
$-27.61\%$ at $+200$ basis points against the $-27.70\%$ disclosed and recovering
the non-monotonicity of the downward shocks. The deposit decay consistent with that
disclosure implies an uninsured half-life of 3.59~years, a figure that absorbs the
bank's unmodelled swap book and any asset-side bias and is therefore a residual rather
than an estimate. The range around it is the result: at a two-year half-life the same
balance sheet shows $-62.47\%$ rather than $-27.61\%$, and across the grid of deposit
assumptions the figure spans $-122\%$ to $+21\%$, against a documented cross-bank mean
behavioural deposit maturity near two years. Both banks
breach the 15\% supervisory outlier screen in every year tested and carried
comparable unrecognised losses, \$15,152m against \$14,138m; on the run model only
SVB crosses a threshold, at 67.5\% of uninsured deposits and through liquidity rather
than solvency. Results are deterministic re-solves rather than statistical estimates,
and no causal claim is made about the March 2023 failure.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability statement}
Every input to this study is public. The four annual reports are available from the
U.S.\ Securities and Exchange Commission's EDGAR system at the accession numbers
given in the reference list, and copies are preserved in the repository below. Par
yield curves are published by the U.S.\ Department of the Treasury and the short-rate
and mortgage-rate series by the Federal Reserve Bank of St.\ Louis; both are
redistributable and the retrieval code is included. The two Basel Committee standards
are freely available from the Bank for International Settlements at the URLs given,
and are cited rather than redistributed.

\section*{Code availability}
The complete analysis code, the extracted filing tables, the model outputs and the
figures are available at \url{https://github.com/soham-parundekar/svb-interest-rate-and-run-risk}.
Results are deterministic: every Monte Carlo draw derives from a seed recorded in the
configuration file, and the same seed is reused across rate shocks so that
differences between scenarios measure curvature rather than sampling noise. A single
command reproduces the pipeline from the committed filings.
